## Highlights

- **Catches more slurs.** The built-in slur list now also has the spellings the speech model often writes for some
  slurs, plus two plural forms it was missing. On test sentences it fully mutes 93 of 176 slurs at 600 ms instead
  of 82 (53 instead of 47 at 500 ms), with no extra mutes in 76 minutes of everyday talk. One of the new entries,
  "dike", is also a word for a flood bank: if you talk about those, add it to the **Allow list** in the Banned
  words tab.
- **No click when a word is caught late.** If a mute arrives after the word has started playing, the sound now
  fades out over 5 ms instead of cutting off with a click. In OBS this needs the new plugin: the Setup tab now
  says when an older one is installed and offers **Update**.

## Fixes and improvements

- Switching the speech model or where it runs no longer keeps the old model loaded. Each switch used to hold
  about 300 MB of video memory until you restarted Bleepline.
- If base.en wasn't downloaded yet, tiny.en filled in and was saved as your choice. Now it only fills in, and
  Bleepline goes back to base.en by itself once you download it.
- Cleaning a long recording needs far less memory: 3 hours with 6 audio tracks went from about 4 GB to under
  1 GB. It uses about 2 GB of temporary disk space instead, deleted when it's done.
- In OBS mode, the note about slow checks goes by the delay set in your Bleepline filters instead of assuming
  500 ms.
- A setting edited by hand in settings.json with the wrong kind of value goes back to its default instead of
  causing errors.

## Upgrading

Run **Bleepline-Setup.exe**. It updates 1.3.0 and older in place and keeps your models, settings and word lists.

- If you use OBS: close OBS, then click **Update** next to OBS plugin in the Setup tab. The installer never
  touches OBS's folder, so the plugin is updated from the app.

## Known limitations

- It lowers the risk of a strike but can't guarantee every word is caught. Most of the wait is the word itself,
  so delays under 400 ms catch far less (about 1 in 4 swear words at 300 ms), and mumbled speech, accents and loud
  game audio make recognition harder.
- Slurs are caught less often than swear words: in tests about half are fully muted at 600 ms, against about 4 in
  5 swear words. The speech model often hears a slur as an everyday word, which no word list can catch.
- The speed and game numbers are from one PC (RTX 3070 Ti, 16-thread processor). With fewer CPU cores, one core
  is a bigger share of the processor.
- In OBS, sources that play a clip and then go quiet (alerts, media files) hold the last part of each clip in the
  filter until they play again. Desktop Audio, microphones and games aren't affected.
- The downloads aren't code-signed yet, so Windows may say *"Windows protected your PC"*: click
  **More info → Run anyway**.
