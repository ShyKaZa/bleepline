## Highlights

- **Word lists made for voice chat.** The built-in list used to be a text-chat list that also muted everyday
  game talk ("kill", "sniper", "god", "stupid") and words like "gay" or "lesbian". It's now split into lists
  you can switch on and off: **strong swearing**, **slurs** and **sexual words** (on), **mild words** like
  hell, damn and crap (off), and the old list as **strict** (off). "he'll" no longer counts as "hell".
- **New installer** (Inno Setup): 70 MB instead of 112 MB, with a dark wizard. It closes a running Bleepline
  before updating, and its uninstaller removes only the files it installed (the old one deleted the whole
  install folder, which was a risk if you picked a shared folder).
- **Update check**: a link appears at the top of the window when a new version is out. The installer asks
  whether to turn it on; it's one request a day to GitHub's public release list and sends nothing about you.
- **Honest numbers and a safer delay**: the Live filter tab shows what each delay catches in tests, and a
  **Safer: 800 ms** button. At 600 ms about 6 in 10 swear words are muted completely and 85% of their sound;
  at 800 ms about 8 in 10 and 93%.

## Fixes and improvements

- Downloads (models, GPU runtime) continue where they stopped after a dropped connection, retry for about
  five minutes, and are checked against Hugging Face's and PyPI's checksums.
- Opening Bleepline twice brings the open window to the front instead of starting a second copy.
- The window fits smaller laptop screens; tabs scroll when they don't fit.
- Cleaning a recording is about twice as fast and frees the GPU memory afterwards.
- Settings and word lists are saved safely (a crash mid-save can't leave a broken file).
- The OBS filter explains that OBS's Render Delay goes up to 500 ms (stack two for more).
- Third-party licenses now also credit Intel oneMKL and oneDNN, which are inside the speech engine; NVIDIA's
  cuDNN loader is no longer bundled (GPU mode doesn't need it).

## Upgrading

Run **Bleepline-Setup.exe**. It updates 1.1 in place (or removes Voice Guard 1.0) and keeps your models,
settings and word lists. Your own words and allow list are kept as they are; check **Banned words** to pick
which built-in lists you want.

## Known limitations

- It lowers the risk of a strike but can't guarantee every word is caught: the start of a word can slip
  through at short delays, and mumbled speech, accents and loud game audio make recognition harder.
- In OBS, sources that play a clip and then go quiet (alerts, media files) hold the last part of each clip
  in the filter until they play again. Desktop Audio, microphones and games aren't affected.
- The downloads aren't code-signed yet, so Windows may say *"Windows protected your PC"*: click
  **More info → Run anyway**.
