## Highlights

- **Catches far more swear words, sooner.** Live filtering now uses the **base.en** speech model on **GPU + CPU**:
  the graphics card turns the audio into features and the processor writes the words down. On 101 swear words in
  87 real recordings it fully mutes 51 at 400 ms, 68 at 500 ms (OBS's Render Delay limit) and 79 at 600 ms.
  1.2's engine muted 25 at 400 ms and 45 at 600 ms.
- **Easy on your game.** Next to a stand-in game that keeps the GPU at 100%, the game loses about 6% of its speed
  (31–34% with everything on the GPU). It uses about one CPU core while someone talks, about 6% of a 16-thread
  processor, and nothing while no one speaks.
- **Choose where it runs** (Options → Live filtering runs on): **GPU + CPU** (recommended), **CPU only** (the game
  loses nothing, but it catches fewer: 52 at 500 ms, 70 at 600 ms), or **GPU only**, for a second PC that only
  streams.

## Fixes and improvements

- It checks what was said every 50 ms instead of every 100 ms (CPU only stays at 100 ms), and it works out exact
  word timings only when a check hears a banned word: the same mutes, with less work per check.
- The note saying checks are slow waits for about 5 seconds of slow checks and appears once per session. It used
  to appear during bursts of swearing with no game running.
- The "caught late" note now says to raise the delay if it keeps happening. It goes by the speech model's own
  timing of the word, which is right about 5 times in 6.
- The Live filter tab shows what each delay catches with the new engine, in OBS mode too.

## Upgrading

Run **Bleepline-Setup.exe**. It updates 1.2 or 1.1 in place and keeps your models, settings and word lists.

- If you used 1.2's live defaults (tiny.en, on the CPU), they move once to base.en on GPU + CPU. Anything else you
  picked stays.
- With an NVIDIA GPU, GPU + CPU needs the GPU runtime (Setup tab, 553 MB). Without it, everything runs on the CPU.
- Without an NVIDIA GPU, base.en runs on the CPU and uses about one core while someone talks. If that's too much,
  pick tiny.en in the Live filter tab: it needs about half as much, but catches far fewer words.
- If base.en isn't downloaded yet, Bleepline keeps using tiny.en. Download base.en in the Models tab, then pick it
  in the Live filter tab.

## Known limitations

- It lowers the risk of a strike but can't guarantee every word is caught. Most of the wait is the word itself,
  so delays under 400 ms catch far less (about 1 in 4 at 300 ms), and mumbled speech, accents and loud game audio
  make recognition harder.
- The speed and game numbers are from one PC (RTX 3070 Ti, 16-thread processor). With fewer CPU cores, one core
  is a bigger share of the processor.
- In OBS, sources that play a clip and then go quiet (alerts, media files) hold the last part of each clip in the
  filter until they play again. Desktop Audio, microphones and games aren't affected.
- The downloads aren't code-signed yet, so Windows may say *"Windows protected your PC"*: click
  **More info → Run anyway**.
